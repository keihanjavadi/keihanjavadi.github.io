Academic Profile

Bridging Macro Turbulence with Nanoscale Dynamics

As a Mechanical Engineering graduate from Sharif University of Technology, my work centers on the fundamental mechanics of fluids and surfaces across vastly different length scales.

At the macroscopic scale, I designed and fabricated an acrylic water channel flow loop from the ground up to investigate how near-wall turbulent coherent structures (quasi-streamwise vortices, ejections, and streaks) can be systematically manipulated using 16K SLA-printed passive microgrooves, achieving up to 28.6% drag reduction.

At the atomic scale, as first and corresponding author of our upcoming Elsevier research, I carry out extensive molecular dynamics (MD) simulations in LAMMPS to uncover how continuous lateral width gradients on bimetallic substrates rectify chaotic Brownian motion into directed, long-range transport for molecular nanomachines.

I combine mathematical formulation, parametric CAD modeling (SolidWorks, OpenSCAD), high-precision additive manufacturing, and high-performance computing (LAMMPS, Python, MATLAB) to bridge theory with rigorous physical experiments.

Using Coherent Structures to Reduce Drag Force: Experimental Investigation of Passive Microgrooves & Bio-Inspired Shark Denticles in Duct Flow

Skin-friction drag in wall-bounded turbulent flows sustains massive energetic losses. This investigation fabricated a dedicated rectangular water channel ($w=51.2\,\text{mm}, h=6.4\,\text{mm}, D_h \approx 11.4\,\text{mm}$, aspect ratio $8$) operating at $Re_{D_h} = 7.2\times 10^3 - 1.7\times 10^4$ to systematically test 16K SLA-printed biomimetic shark denticles and microgrooves.

Critical Experimental Discoveries

  • Viscous Sublayer Confinement ($h_d^+ < 5$): Confining rectangular microgrooves ($w_g = 0.35\,\text{mm}, s=0.65\,\text{mm}, h_g = 0.20\,\text{mm}$) inside the viscous sublayer ($\delta_v \approx 57-121\,\mu\text{m}$) pinned quasi-streamwise vortices and damped crossflow fluctuations, yielding 21.8% to 28.6% drag reduction.
  • The Shark Denticle Penalty: Rigid full-height denticles ($180\,\mu\text{m}$) protruded into the buffer/log layer ($y^+ \approx 15-30$), inducing heavy form drag penalties (+25% to +35% pressure drop), proving the crucial necessity of flexible anchoring in living organisms.
  • Cassie-Baxter Superhydrophobic State: Optical sessile drop goniometry revealed an apparent contact angle jump from $48.9^\circ$ (flat acrylic) to $119.9^\circ$ (+71° jump!) purely by trapping micro-air plastrons in rectangular grooves.

Directional Transport and Geometric Confinement of Molecular Nanomachines on Bimetallic Width-Gradient Substrates

Controlling the diffusive motion of synthetic molecular nanomachines remains a premier frontier in nanotechnology and targeted nanomedicine. This study utilized classical molecular dynamics (LAMMPS, NVT canonical ensemble, Nosé-Hoover thermostat, Velocity-Verlet, 1 fs step) to evaluate 6 distinct nanomachine architectures on bimetallic Au/Ag width-gradient substrates across 75 K to 600 K.

Key Simulation & Theoretical Breakthroughs

  • Rectification via Negative Width Gradients (NG): While uniform (ZG) and diverging (PG) gold tracks suffer from severe lateral wander, the converging negative gradient (NG, 135°) suppresses angular velocity and rectifies Brownian diffusion into directed, long-range transport.
  • Sliding-to-Rolling Dynamics: Thermal excitation initiates a distinct mechanical transition from atomic sliding to pure rolling. On NG surfaces, geometric confinement drives early transition and preserves directional stability up to 600 K.
  • Structural Flexibility vs. Rigidity: Lightweight, flexible chassis excel in cryogenic/low-temperature regimes (75–150 K), whereas structurally balanced, rigid configurations dominate high-temperature environments.

Sharif University of Technology B.Sc. Thesis

Using Coherent Structures to Reduce Drag Force

The Physical Problem

Skin-friction drag in wall-bounded turbulent flows is a primary energy drain across aeronautical, marine, and pipe transport systems. Near-wall turbulence is driven by a self-sustaining cycle: elongated quasi-streamwise vortices inclined at $8^\circ{-}10^\circ$ ($y^+ \approx 10{-}40$), low-speed fluid streaks ($\lambda^+ \approx 100$), and energetic sweep ($Q_4$) and ejection ($Q_2$) events that steepen wall shear stress:

$$\tau_{\text{turb}} = -\rho \overline{u' v'} > 0$$

This project investigated whether microstructured surfaces could mechanically suppress these quasi-streamwise vortices and lift turbulent ejections away from the surface without suffering the detrimental form drag that plagues macroscopic roughness.

Why Viscous Sublayer Confinement ($h_d^+ < 5$) Matters

Classical boundary layer theory dictates that within $y^+ < 5$, viscous damping dominates. If microgrooves or denticles remain submerged strictly within this layer, they impede spanwise crossflow fluctuations ($w'$) and prevent quasi-streamwise vortices from sweeping down to the wall. Conversely, when denticles extended into the buffer zone ($y^+ \approx 15-30$), they operated as bluff bodies, generating localized separation and massive 25%–35% form drag penalties.

Elsevier Research Paper • First Author

Directional Transport of Molecular Nanomachines

Abstract

"Molecular nanomachines hold immense potential for applications in targeted drug delivery, molecular transport, and nanoscale manipulation; however, achieving precise directional control over their diffusive motion remains a significant challenge. While previous research has extensively examined substrate topography and wheel composition, the specific influence of continuous width gradients on nanomachine mobility across varying thermal conditions is not fully understood. This study employs classical molecular dynamics (MD) simulations to investigate the performance of diverse nanomachines—including Nanocar, Miller nanocar, Nanotruck, Angled Nanocar, Nano-cooper, and Nano-caterpillar—on bimetallic substrates featuring a uniform gold path (Zero Gradient, ZG) alongside positive (PG) and negative (NG) width-gradient gold paths on a silver base, evaluated from 75 K to 600 K. The results reveal that although elevated temperatures generally enhance mobility by driving the dynamic transition from sliding to rolling, uniform and positive gradient surfaces exhibit delayed transitions and substantial lateral deviations. In contrast, the negative gradient surface significantly improves directional stability, minimizes angular velocity, and facilitates long-range displacement through synergistic geometric confinement and optimized surface energetics."

Research Highlights

  • Continuous negative width gradients rectify diffusive Brownian motion of diverse molecular nanomachines into directed, long-range transport.
  • Elevated thermal excitation accelerates the dynamical transition from atomic sliding to rolling locomotion.
  • Negative gradient tracks suppress parasitic angular velocity and eliminate lateral drift at elevated temperatures.
  • Structural flexibility confers superior mobility at cryogenic temperatures (75–150 K), whereas rigid architectures excel at high temperatures (400–600 K).

Evaluated Nanomachine Architectures

Six distinct designs spanning chassis flexibilities, masses, and wheel configurations were modeled:

Nanocar

Baseline four-wheel design featuring rigid axle connectivity and symmetric fullerene wheels.

Miller Nanocar

Flexible chassis architecture with rotational freedom around central covalent linkages.

Nanotruck

Higher mass, structurally reinforced chassis engineered for cargo transport capabilities.

Angled Nanocar

Asymmetric wheel inclination testing susceptibility to induced lateral torque.

Nano-cooper

Compact, rigid high-mobility chassis design with lowered center of mass.

Nano-caterpillar

Segmented multi-body architecture designed to navigate irregular surface potential barriers.

Kinematic Rectification & Trajectory Stability

On uniform (ZG) and diverging (PG) substrates, nanomachines undergo frequent lateral deviations, random reorientations, and erratic angular acceleration. In contrast, on the Negative Gradient (NG) substrate, the converging gold boundaries exert an energetic restoring force: as the vehicle approaches the silver boundary, the difference in interfacial adhesion energetically penalizes lateral escape, confining the nanomachine to smooth, unidirectionally oriented locomotion along the track axis.
